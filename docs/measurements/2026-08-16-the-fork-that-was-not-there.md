# The fork this repository published, and the absence nobody measured

**2026-08-16.** `out/textual/significator-series-rules.jsonl` was published carrying a
**fork**: that the two located copies place the rule for reading the ascending node's degrees
differently, and that a consumer reversing the node when ranking the significator series is
therefore following one copy against the other.

⛔⛔⛔ **The fork was not there.** The second copy states the rule where the series is
founded, in the same commentary, two paragraphs below the passage the reading was formed
from. The verdict is now **corroborated**, on all five rules.

---

## 1. What was published, and what refutes it

The withdrawn reading rested on one observation, and the observation is true: at the sutra
founding the series, the second copy gives a *different* ground for leaving the descending
node out — that its degrees always equal the ascending node's. The recorder read that and
concluded the copy did not invoke the reversal there.

⭐⭐⭐ **A different reason found is not the absence of the reason you were looking for.**

Two paragraphs further down the same commentary, that copy states:

> in determining the head of the series the node must certainly be considered — if it holds
> the greatest traversed degrees (for this, subtract its exact degrees from thirty and take
> the remainder), it too can head the series

Located, occurring **exactly once**, at offset **+2 111** inside the commentary to sutra 11,
where the next sutra begins at **+3 066**. ⛔ It is not a marginal or inferable reading: it
names the head of the series, and it gives the subtraction.

⇒ Both copies state the reversal **at the founding sutra** and **at the later determination**.
Four corroborated became five.

## 2. How the error was made, and why nothing caught it

⚠ The session that published the fork ran the refusing direction and passed it **5/5**. Five
fragments were located, each occurring exactly once; five deliberate mutations of them —
a word inserted, a fragment shortened until it occurred 286 times, a passage made
unresolvable, a fragment resolved against the wrong copy — were each refused.

⭐⭐⭐ **None of that touched the claim built on top of them.** *A refusal control proves the
fragments you wrote are located; it says nothing about the finding you assembled out of them.*
The fork was a **presence and an absence**, the presence was measured to the hilt, and the
absence was never measured at all.

⛔ Worse, the file's own neighbouring refusal — `extent_of_the_copy_is_a_lower_bound` — says
no absence may be measured over that copy. The fork row asserted one in prose, on the same
page, in the row beside it.

## 3. ⚠ And the obvious check would have confirmed the error

This is the half worth carrying, because it is where the instinctive repair fails.

Measured over the founding commentary, in the passage that **does** state the rule:

| Spelling searched | Hits in that passage |
|---|---|
| the ordinary word for the reversal (`विपरीत`) | **0** |
| the copy's term for the degrees the rule reads (`भुक्तांश`) | 1 |
| the copy's word for the subtraction (`घटाकर`) | 1 |
| four further spellings of reversed order | 0 |

⛔ **An absence search over that passage, in the obvious alphabet, returns zero and confirms
the fork.** The copy states the rule there as **arithmetic** and elsewhere as **description**,
and a recorder assembling an alphabet from the *idea* never reaches the arithmetic.

⭐⭐⭐ The alphabet that catches it is the one read off **the same rule as the other copy
states it**, carrying form by carrying form. ⚠ *A walker that knows one carrying form has
measured the wrong subject* — the same sentence as the boundary-marker defect before it, one
level up, and this time about a claim rather than about a count.

⇒ A test asserts this limit **rather than pretending to escape it**: given only the ordinary
word, the mechanism accepts an absence that is false. What the row carries instead is the
alphabet itself, so a reader can see what was not looked for.

## 4. What is armed

* **`Fork` refuses at write time** unless its absence half is established over a passage
  bounded by two landmarks that each resolve exactly once. ⭐ Run against the withdrawn fork
  it refuses it **and names the two words that refute it**.
* **`PassageAbsence`** — an absence bounded by a **located passage** rather than by an extent.
  ⭐ That bound is measured, so it does not depend on how much of the work the copy contains:
  it is the one absence that may honestly be taken over a copy whose extent is a lower bound.
* ⛔ **Every spelling must be attested somewhere in that copy.** A spelling occurring zero
  times in the whole rendering reports the recorder's vocabulary as the passage's silence,
  and does so with a zero that looks like evidence. Seven boundary-marker spellings were
  guessed in a previous session; six matched nothing and the one that matched was right by
  luck.
* A fork naming one copy on both sides is refused — a copy disagreeing with itself is a
  different finding and is already recorded as one.

## 5. ⭐⭐ No single offset describes the two copies' numbering

The withdrawn reading's *stated reason* was that the copies "attach it to different sutras".
They do print it under different numbers — 50 and 53 — and that difference is **not** a
difference of doctrine.

Measured against the neighbouring sutra both copies print:

| | first copy | second copy | offset |
|---|---|---|---|
| the anchor (`Brahmani sanau…` / `ब्रह्मणि शनो…`) | 48 | 50 | **2** |
| the sutra under comparison (`Rahuyoge vipareetam` / `राहुयोगे विपरीतम्`) | 50 | 53 | **3** |
| the sutra after it (`Vivade balee` / `विवादे बली`) | 52 | 54 | **2** |

⛔⛔ The offset is 2 on **either side** and 3 **at** the sutra in question, because the copies
do not merely renumber — they **reorder**, one printing a sutra before the pair that the other
prints after. ⭐ **A recorder carrying the neighbour's offset lands on a different sutra
entirely**, which is how a difference of numbering becomes a published difference of doctrine.

⇒ Identity of place is **refused**, under a new reason
`place_in_the_work_not_established_across_copies`. ⚠ Settling it from the sutras' own words
would mean matching a roman transliteration against a Devanagari line, and no authority for
that is held here.

## 6. The re-emission, and its controls

The held file was copied aside and digest-verified **first** — `out/` is git-ignored, so a
copy made first is the only diff baseline that exists. Against it:

* **19 rows byte-identical**, including all five rule rows, the four other corroborations,
  the absence and every standing refusal — ⭐ **no value drifted anywhere else**;
* **1 row changed** — the fork, now a corroboration;
* **5 rows added** — the correction, the alignment, one refusal, two controls.

⭐ The comparator was then driven off its own value rather than trusted: the file against
itself reports 0 changed; the held file against itself 0 changed; perturbing one untouched
rule row moves the count 1 → 2; and ⚠ **reverting only the fork row moves it 1 → 0**, which
is what makes the single change load-bearing rather than vacuous. **4/4.**

Byte-identical on re-run. All **5** controls held, including the two new ones — the second of
which holds precisely when the passage is *not* silent, so it fails the day the withdrawal
stops being justified.

## 7. What this does not settle

* ⛔ The **first copy's notes as that translator's own words** still rest on one copy. Only a
  second printing of that translation can witness them. ⚠ **And the candidate table in the
  previous measurement rejected exactly that copy** — "a fuller printing of the same
  translator … not a second witness at all" — which was true of the question then being asked
  and stopped being true when the refusal was rewritten narrower. ⭐ *A rejection recorded
  against a question that has since been replaced is not a rejection any more*, and the
  candidate list was never re-read after the question changed.
* ⛔ The sutras **as either copy prints them** are still not citable.
* ⛔ The **absence** in the fixture is still measured over the first copy only.
* ⚠ The reversal is now corroborated at both loci; ⛔ **whether the two copies' later loci are
  the same sutra is refused**, not answered.
