# The alphabet that marked the wrong hand — 2026-08-18

> ⛔⛔⛔ **The second-printing test has been asked four times. The reason no candidate ever
> passed it is that no copy of this work can: four of the twelve spellings it is scored on
> mark the translator, the primary text, or the reader's own damage.**

The standing test, unchanged for three sessions, was: acquire a printing of this translation,
search it for the twelve spellings by which the copy in hand marks its second commenting
hand, and require **zero over the whole copy**, over a copy shown readable in the script
those spellings are written in.

A third candidate was acquired. It is the first that is demonstrably readable — 205 055
Latin letters and nothing else. It fails, carrying nine of the twelve. And it is the first
candidate whose failure explains the other two.

---

## 1. The copy this repository held as mute is readable, and it is the same copy

A previous session acquired a printing of this work as a PDF of 219 scanned page images
whose rendering carries no text at all, and recorded of it that it establishes

> *nothing whatever, and that includes its own identity* — that the work, the translator and
> the printing were known here only from the name a host gives the file, which is a fact
> about a host and not about a book.

A public archive distributes a copy of that printing whose bytes are **identical**:

| | |
|---|---|
| copy bytes | 13 905 548 |
| SHA-1 | `cdf112dfa3d061658daf5e55a4c2e35337db5f5a` |
| checked against | the archive's own published file manifest, and the digest of the copy in this repository's cache |

and beside it a machine reading of that same copy carrying **205 055 Latin letters**.

⭐⭐⭐ **A copy that renders to nothing in one reader is not a copy that says nothing.** The
muteness was a property of the rendering this repository produced. The identity the earlier
row said the copy could never attest is on its **first page** — it was never unavailable, it
was unread. The correction is a row in the artifact, not a silent edit.

What that first page says, each resolving exactly once:

* a title page — *By Prof. B SURYANARAIN RAO*, and beneath it *REVISED AND EDITED BY HIS
  GRANDSON B\* V. RAMAN*;
* an imprint, as this reading damaged it — *THIRD E DITTO*;
* a signed foreword — *the third and revised edition of the English Translation* — dated
  *Bangalore. 16-11-1949*.

⚠ The blank rendering is **kept**. It is still the only honest place in this repository to
hold the *renders-to-nothing* control, and it is now also the place to hold the sharper one:
that a mute rendering is a fact about a reader.

## 2. ⛔⛔⛔ The test would have rejected the printing it was built to find

The twelve spellings were read off the copy in hand rather than guessed — which is this
file's rule, and it is the right rule. ⭐⭐ **But the copy they were read off prints both
hands on the same pages, and the alphabet inherited both.**

Checked against four passages, each resolving exactly once in the third edition:

| spelling | also marks | the passage that refutes it |
|---|---|---|
| `Prof.` | the translator on his own title page | *By Prof. B SURYANARAIN RAO* |
| `Professor` | the translator on the half-title of the translation | *ENGLISH TRANSLATION BY Professor B. SURYANARAIN RAO* |
| `my work` | **the translation of the first sutra of the work** | *I shall now explain my work for the benefit of the readers* |
| `*` | the machine reading's own damage | *REVISED AND EDITED BY HIS GRANDSON B\* V. RAMAN* |

The third of these is the sharpest. *I shall now explain my work* is the translated **first
aphorism** — the primary text speaking — and it resolves exactly once in **every copy of this
translation held here**, the copy all five rules resolve into included.

⇒ A printing free of the second hand still contains sutra 1 and still names its translator.
**Carrying none of the twelve spellings is a condition no copy of this work can satisfy**,
and the test as written must reject the very printing it was built to find.

⭐ Reading a spelling off a copy establishes that it is **attested**. It does not establish
that it is **discriminating**, and only the second is what a marker alphabet needs.

## 3. ⭐⭐⭐ One defective entry was all that denied a copy of pure noise a clean pass

The previous session measured the library scan — a machine reading of an English printing
carrying **zero Latin letters** — and recorded that it *misses a clean pass by one punctuation
mark*, the twelfth spelling being the printed asterisk at 128 hits.

Scored on the **eleven spellings that are words**, that copy scores **0 of 11**: a perfect
pass, over a rendering that cannot express English.

⛔ A recorder who tidied the asterisk out of the alphabet — correctly, since it is not a word,
carries no script, and marks nothing — would have published *a printing free of the second
hand* on the strength of noise.

⚠ **The repository was not in fact exposed**: the script refusal armed the same session
rejects that copy before a single spelling is counted. ⭐ But the *score* was never the
protection, and this file recorded the count as having come close to working. It was never
working.

Three machine readings of the third edition are now held. They are readings of **one
edition**, tied by a fragment resolving exactly once in each: *my revered grandfather late
Professor*. ⛔ Of four candidate ties tried, only that one qualifies — the other three are on
the page in all three readings and the readings spell them differently.

⇒ **A clean pass on this test, had one ever been obtained, would have measured the reader and
not the printing.** This is a second defect, independent of the first: the test is unreachable
by construction, and were it reachable it would not be sound.

## 5. ⚠ The disclaimer was itself revised between printings

The previous session published a contradiction in the fifth edition's signed foreword. Both
halves are in the **third** edition too, six years earlier, a paragraph apart — and the
disclaimer's verb changed between them:

| edition | the disclaimer | the sentence that contradicts it |
|---|---|---|
| third (1949) | *I have not **interfered** with either the translation or the notes as given by Prof. Rao* | *The Translation herewith presented has been thoroughly revised by me* |
| fifth (1955) | *I have not **meddled** with either the translation or the notes as given by Prof. Rao* | *The Translation herewith presented has been thoroughly revised by me* |

⭐⭐⭐ **The sentence claiming the translation was not altered is itself altered between
printings**, and the contradiction is not a slip made once — it survived its author re-setting
his own foreword for a new edition.

⚠ **Weighed, not asserted.** *Interfered* and *meddled* are not a confusion an optical reader
makes, and the third edition's own rendering spells *meddled* correctly elsewhere — in the
sutra-scoped disclaimer in its body — so the reader that produced *interfered* in the foreword
could read *meddled* in the same file. ⛔ That is evidence, not proof: these are two scans as
well as two readings, and nothing here separates a reviser's second thought from a
compositor's.

⛔ It establishes **nothing about what was actually changed**. A reviser's own account of what
he changed is not evidence of what he changed, and that holds twice as hard for an account he
revised. `revised_printing_cannot_witness_the_unrevised_words` **stands**.

## 6. What is armed

Each refuses a row that can actually be built, on a copy held.

* **`MarkerAlphabet`** — an alphabet claiming to mark one hand must not fire on located
  passages of material it does not mark. Run against the twelve it **refuses and names all
  four**. The measurement, `alphabet_contamination`, is always computable and is published;
  only the licence is withheld.
* **`AbsenceAcrossReadings`** — an absence claimed of a *printing* must hold in **every held
  reading** of it, and the readings must be tied to one edition by a fragment resolving
  exactly once in each. Run against the three readings it **refuses and names the four that
  flip**. ⛔ It refuses a single reading outright: one reading agrees with itself perfectly.
* **`reading_disagreement`** — the measurement behind it, published rather than raised.

⚠ **What is not armed**: nothing here narrows the twelve spellings to the eight that do
discriminate, and no absence is re-scored on a narrowed alphabet. Choosing a new alphabet
changes what the published claim *means*, and that is not a repair to make silently in the
same session that found the defect.

## 7. Whether an earlier printing is reachable — the open question, answered

⛔ **Not reached.** Every reachable digitised printing of this translation carries the same
second hand's signed foreword. The earliest is the third edition, already revised by that
hand; the copy that names itself the fifth is 1955. An earlier printing is established to
have **existed** — a third edition presupposes a first — and no digitised one was found.

⚠ A statement about what was found, never about what exists.

⭐ And it would not have settled anything if one had been found, because the alphabet it would
have been scored on does not mark the hand it names. **The standing question was not open for
want of a candidate.**

⛔ One candidate was rejected before acquisition: a public archive item titled *Jaimini
Sutras, 1911 Edition* is a bundle of Mīmāṃsā works — the philosophical Jaimini, a different
author and a different book. Right name, wrong work.

## 8. The re-emission and its controls

Held file copied aside and digest-verified **first**. Compared **by row identity, never by
position** — ten rows were added, and a positional diff would call the whole tail changed.

**40 byte-identical · 0 changed · 10 added · 0 removed.** Every rule, every corroboration,
the earlier correction, the alignment, the hands row, the naming row, the foreword row, the
absence and all standing refusals untouched. Byte-identical on re-run. **17/17 artifact
controls held**, five of them new.

⭐ Comparator driven off its own value, **5/5**:

| control | expected | measured |
|---|---|---|
| held vs itself | 0 changed | 0 changed, 40 byte-identical |
| new vs itself | 0 changed | 0 changed, 50 byte-identical |
| an untouched **rule** row perturbed | changed 0 → 1 | 1 |
| a **carried control** row perturbed | byte-identical 40 → 39 | 39 |
| a row dropped | removed 0 → 1 | 1 |

⚠ **The revert control that has been sharpest in previous sessions was unavailable again,
because `changed` is 0.** The perturbation controls stand in for it, and they are what prove
the comparator is not simply always reporting zero.

## 9. What was deliberately not done

* ⛔ **No machine reading was produced here.** Every rendering used is its distributor's; an
  absence over our own optical reading would be an absence over our own mistakes.
* ⛔ **The five rules and five corroborations were not touched** — all still byte-identical.
* ⛔ **The twelve spellings were not narrowed**, and no absence was re-scored — see armed,
  above.
* ⛔ **The blank 219-page rendering was not dropped** from the source table.
* ⛔ **Not pushed.** The owner asked for a local commit only.
* ⛔ **No hand-over into the consuming tree.** Nothing read from or written to it.
* ⛔ **R5 was not run**; **R4 still has no generator**; the flag-attestation artifacts and the
  registry rows were not touched.
* ⛔ The conventions time-offset artifact was **left alone** on the owner's instruction: its
  blocker is a decision on the consumer's side that has not been made, and re-emitting against
  a schema nobody has ruled on would be guessing.
